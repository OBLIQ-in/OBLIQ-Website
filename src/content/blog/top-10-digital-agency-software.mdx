Every agency eventually hits the same wall: work is scattered across inboxes, chat threads, spreadsheets and someone's memory. The fix is not "more software" — it is the _right_ software, set up so each tool has one clear job.

Below are ten tools we see small and growing agencies rely on, grouped by the job they do. You will not need all of them on day one. Use the list as a map: find the category that hurts most right now and start there.

## How we picked

A tool made the list if it is:

- **Proven** — widely used by agencies, with a track record beyond a single launch.
- **Easy to start** — a team can get value from it in an afternoon, not a quarter.
- **Friendly to clients** — it lets you share work with people outside your team without friction.
- **Connected** — it plays nicely with the rest of your stack.

Pricing changes often, so check each vendor's site for current plans. Most offer a free tier or trial, which is the best way to decide.

## Projects and planning

### 1. Notion

Notion is a flexible workspace for documents, wikis and simple databases. Agencies use it for their internal handbook, project briefs, meeting notes and lightweight client portals. Its strength is flexibility; its weakness is the same — agree on a structure early or it becomes a maze.

**Best for:** documenting how you work, and keeping briefs and notes in one place.

### 2. Asana

Asana turns projects into tasks with owners, due dates and dependencies, with list, board and timeline views. It shines once you have repeatable projects: build a template for "website launch" or "brand sprint" once and reuse it for every client.

**Best for:** teams running several projects at once who need clear ownership.

### 3. ClickUp

ClickUp covers similar ground to Asana but packs more into one app — docs, goals, whiteboards and time tracking alongside tasks. It suits teams who want one tool for almost everything and are willing to spend time configuring it.

**Best for:** agencies that prefer one highly customisable workspace over several focused tools.

## Design and creative

### 4. Figma

Figma is the default for interface and brand design work, and its real superpower for agencies is collaboration. Clients can view files and leave comments directly on a design, which beats a PDF and a long email thread every time.

**Best for:** design teams, and anyone who needs clear, visual feedback from clients.

### 5. Loom

Loom records quick videos of your screen and face. A three-minute walkthrough of a design or a report often replaces a thirty-minute meeting, and clients can watch it whenever suits them.

**Best for:** presenting work asynchronously and explaining decisions without a call.

## Communication

### 6. Slack

Slack keeps internal conversation out of email and organised by channel. Many agencies also invite clients into shared channels for faster back-and-forth. Set expectations about response times, or chat quickly becomes an always-on obligation.

**Best for:** fast internal communication and close collaboration with retainer clients.

### 7. Google Workspace

Email, calendar, documents, spreadsheets and shared drives in one account. It is unglamorous, but it is where contracts, proposals and reports usually live, and nearly every client already knows how to use it.

**Best for:** the everyday backbone — mail, files and documents you share with clients.

## Sales and clients

### 8. HubSpot CRM

A CRM keeps track of every lead, conversation and deal so nothing slips through the cracks. HubSpot's free CRM is a sensible starting point: it logs emails, tracks deals through stages and grows into marketing tools if you need them later.

**Best for:** agencies that want a clear view of their sales pipeline.

## Time and money

### 9. Harvest

Harvest combines time tracking with invoicing. Your team logs hours against projects, and you can turn those hours into an invoice in a few clicks. Even on fixed-price work, tracking time tells you which projects actually make money.

**Best for:** measuring profitability per project and billing hourly work.

### 10. Xero

Accounting software like Xero connects to your bank, reconciles transactions and produces the reports your accountant will ask for. QuickBooks is a popular alternative; the best choice often depends on what your accountant already uses in your country.

**Best for:** keeping your books clean and your tax season calm.

## Building your stack

A good stack is small. Here is a sensible order to adopt tools as you grow:

1. **Solo or two people:** Google Workspace, Figma and a time tracker. Keep projects in a simple shared document.
2. **Three to five people:** add a project tool (Asana or ClickUp) and Slack. Start writing your process in Notion.
3. **Five people and up:** add a CRM and proper accounting software, and connect them so data flows without copy-pasting.

> Before adding a new tool, ask one question: which existing tool or habit does this replace? If the answer is "nothing", you probably do not need it yet.

The right stack is the one your team actually uses. Pick fewer tools, set each one up properly, and revisit the list every six months — your needs will change as your agency does.
